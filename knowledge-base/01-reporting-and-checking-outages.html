Reporting and Checking a Power Outage

Brightvale Electric Customer Help Center. How to report an outage, check whether an outage is already known in your area, and understand your estimated restoration time.

How do I report a power outage?

You can report an outage 24 hours a day through the GridGuide chat assistant, on our website, or by calling Customer Service at (800) 555-0100.

To report an outage we only need the ZIP code of the address without power and the last 4 digits of the phone number on the account. We never need your full account number, Social Security number, or any password to report an outage.

If you see a downed power line, sparks, or fire, do not report it through chat. Stay at least 35 feet away and call 911 and our 24-hour Emergency Line at (800) 555-0111 right away.

How do I check if there is already an outage in my area?

GridGuide can check our live outage system using your ZIP code. If an outage is already known in your area, it will tell you how many customers are affected, the cause if known, and the estimated restoration time.

If the outage is already known, you do not need to file another report. Duplicate reports do not speed up restoration. Instead, you can sign up for restoration text alerts so we tell you when power is back.

What should I check before reporting an outage?

  • Check whether your neighbors or streetlights also have no power. If they do have power, the problem may be inside your home.
  • Check your circuit breakers or fuses. A tripped breaker can cut power to part of your home.
  • If only part of your home has no power and your breakers are fine, report it as a partial outage. This can mean a problem with the service line to your home.

What is an estimated restoration time (ETR)?

An estimated restoration time, or ETR, is our best estimate of when power will be restored. It is based on the cause of the outage, the damage our crews find, and how many crews are available.

An ETR is an estimate, not a guarantee. It can change when crews arrive and assess the damage, when more damage is found, or when severe weather makes work unsafe. We update ETRs as soon as we have new information.

During large storms, it can take several hours before crews can assess every area. In that case the ETR may show as "Assessing" until a crew has inspected the damage.

Why does my neighbor have power when I do not?

Homes on the same street can be served by different power lines, transformers, or circuits. Your home may also have damage to its own service line that does not affect your neighbors. If your neighbors have power and you do not, please report your outage so a crew can check your service line.

What happens after I report an outage?

You receive a ticket number, for example BV-48213. Keep it for your records. Our system groups your report with others nearby so crews can find the source of the problem faster.

You can sign up for restoration text alerts to get updates on your outage, including ETR changes and a message when power is restored.
