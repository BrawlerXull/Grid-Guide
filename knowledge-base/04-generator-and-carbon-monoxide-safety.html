Generator and Carbon Monoxide Safety

Brightvale Electric Customer Help Center. How to use a portable or standby generator safely during a power outage and protect your household from carbon monoxide poisoning.

Is it safe to run a generator in my garage?

No. Never run a generator inside your home, garage, basement, crawl space, shed, or any partly enclosed area, even with doors or windows open. Generators produce carbon monoxide (CO), an invisible, odorless gas that can kill within minutes.

Always run portable generators outdoors, at least 20 feet from your home, with the exhaust pointed away from doors, windows, and vents.

How do I connect a generator safely?

  • Plug appliances directly into the generator using heavy-duty outdoor extension cords rated for the load.
  • Never plug a generator into a wall outlet. This is called backfeeding. It can send electricity back into power lines and seriously injure or kill utility workers and neighbors.
  • Permanently installed standby generators must be connected by a licensed electrician using an approved transfer switch.

Carbon monoxide warning signs

Symptoms of carbon monoxide poisoning include headache, dizziness, weakness, nausea, vomiting, chest pain, and confusion. If anyone has these symptoms, or if a CO alarm sounds, get everyone outside to fresh air immediately and call 911.

Install battery-operated or battery-backup carbon monoxide alarms on every level of your home and outside sleeping areas. Test them monthly.

Other generator safety tips

  • Let the generator cool before refueling. Fuel spilled on hot parts can ignite.
  • Store fuel outside your home in approved containers, away from heat sources.
  • Keep the generator dry and do not touch it with wet hands.
  • Never use a gas stove, oven, or charcoal grill to heat your home.
