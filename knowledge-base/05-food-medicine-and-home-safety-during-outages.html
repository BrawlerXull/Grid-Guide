Food, Medicine, and Home Safety During an Outage

Brightvale Electric Customer Help Center. How to keep food and medicine safe, protect your electronics, and stay safe in hot or cold weather while the power is out.

How long will food stay safe in my refrigerator and freezer?

  • Keep refrigerator and freezer doors closed as much as possible.
  • A closed refrigerator keeps food safe for about 4 hours.
  • A full freezer keeps its temperature for about 48 hours. A half-full freezer keeps its temperature for about 24 hours.
  • After 4 hours without power, move perishable food such as meat, dairy, and leftovers into a cooler with ice.
  • When in doubt, throw it out. Never taste food to decide whether it is safe.

What about refrigerated medicine such as insulin?

Some medicines must be kept cold. Ask your pharmacist or doctor how long your medicine can safely stay at room temperature and what to do if it gets too warm. Do not use medicine that looks unusual or has changed color. If you rely on refrigerated medicine, consider enrolling in our Medical Priority program and keep a cooler and ice packs ready.

How do I protect my electronics and appliances?

  • Unplug sensitive electronics such as computers and televisions during an outage to protect them from power surges when power returns.
  • Leave one light switched on so you know when power is restored.
  • Turn off major appliances such as air conditioners, heaters, and ovens, then turn them back on one at a time after power returns.

Staying safe in hot or cold weather

During extended outages in extreme heat or cold, Brightvale County opens cooling and warming centers. GridGuide and our website list open centers during declared storm emergencies. Drink plenty of water in hot weather. In cold weather, dress in layers and close off unused rooms. Never use a gas stove, oven, or grill to heat your home.

Lighting and candles

Use flashlights or battery-powered lanterns instead of candles to reduce the risk of fire. If you must use candles, never leave them unattended.
